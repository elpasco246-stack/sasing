Core Content

1. Spaced practice

Instead of studying one topic for three hours in a single night, split it into shorter sessions spread over several days. Revisiting material just as you start to forget it strengthens memory far more than re-reading it straight away.

A simple pattern: review new material after 1 day, then 3 days, then 1 week.

2. Active recall

Testing yourself is more effective than passively re-reading. Try these:

  • Flashcards – question on one side, answer on the other.
  • Blank-page recall – close the book and write everything you remember.
  • Teach it – explain the idea aloud as if to a friend.

3. Sleep and breaks

Memories are consolidated during sleep, so an all-nighter works against you. During study, short breaks keep attention high.

Show: the 25/5 rhythm

Work for 25 minutes with full focus, then rest for 5 minutes. After four rounds, take a longer break of 15–30 minutes. This is often called the Pomodoro technique.

Show: what makes a good break?

Stand up, stretch, drink water, or look out of a window. Avoid starting a new screen-based distraction that is hard to stop.
